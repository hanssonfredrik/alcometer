How the estimate works

Promilla estimates your blood alcohol level with the Widmark formula. It uses your weight, your sex and the drinks you log. It is a rough guide, not a measurement.

Last updated 31 Oct 2026

The short version

  • Each drink adds to the estimate as soon as you log it.
  • The estimate falls by 0.15 ‰ an hour and stops at zero.
  • Your weight and sex decide how much one drink adds.
  • Food, health and medication are not part of it.

Never use it to decide whether you can drive.

The formula

For each drink, Promilla first works out the grams of pure alcohol:

grams = volume in ml × strength × 0.789

0.789 g per ml is the density of alcohol. The strength is the share of alcohol, so 5% is 0.05. Then the drink adds this much to the level:

added ‰ = grams ÷ (r × weight in kg)

r is the share of your body that holds the alcohol, as Erik Widmark defined it. Promilla uses 0.68 for men and 0.55 for women. Between drinks the level falls by 0.15 ‰ an hour. It never goes below zero, and hours spent at zero don't count against a later drink.

An example

A man who weighs 75 kg has one 33 cl beer at 5%.

Step Result
Alcohol in the beer 330 × 0.05 × 0.789 = 13.0 g
Added to the level 13.0 ÷ (0.68 × 75) = 0.26 ‰
One hour later 0.26 − 0.15 = 0.11 ‰
Estimate reaches zero About 1 hour 40 minutes after the beer

A woman who weighs 60 kg and has the same beer gets 13.0 ÷ (0.55 × 60) = 0.39 ‰.

Standard drinks

A standard drink is 12 g of pure alcohol. Promilla counts your drinks in standard drinks, so different sizes and strengths add up the same way. You set a weekly target in standard drinks, and History and Insights show your weeks against it. Promilla's usual sizes, which you can change:

Drink Size and strength Standard drinks
Wine 15 cl at 12% 1.2
Beer 33 cl at 5% 1.1
Cava 12 cl at 11% 0.9
Liquor 6 cl at 40% 1.6

The levels Promilla shows

Name Estimated level
Zero 0.00 ‰
Light Under 0.20 ‰
Tipsy 0.20 to 0.49 ‰
Drunk 0.50 to 0.99 ‰
Very drunk 1.00 ‰ and above

The names describe the estimate and nothing more. Zero means the estimate has reached 0.00. It doesn't mean the alcohol has left your body.

What the estimate leaves out

  • Time to absorb. Promilla counts a drink in full the moment you log it. Your real level climbs over a while after you drink.
  • Food. A meal slows down how fast alcohol reaches your blood.
  • Your body. Health, medication, age and how often you drink all change how fast you break alcohol down. 0.15 ‰ an hour is an average, and people differ.
  • Your pour. The estimate is only as good as the sizes and strengths you log.

Because of all this, your real level can be quite different from the estimate, higher or lower.

Never use it to decide whether you can drive.

Days and alcohol-free streaks

Promilla's days roll over at 05:00, so a night out that runs past midnight stays in one day. A day with no drinks logged is an alcohol-free day. Several in a row make an alcohol-free streak, with milestones at 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 90 days.
