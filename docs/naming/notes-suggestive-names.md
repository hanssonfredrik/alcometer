Research date: 2026-10-20. 15 candidates proposed: Promille, Permille, Promilla, Mille, Tally, Sipwise, Pourly, Sip Count, Paced/Pacely, Sipline, Lagom, Måtta, Klunk, Glasskoll, Skål, Nightcap, Tonight.

## Does an app with this or a confusingly similar name exist on the App Store or Google Play?

### Takeaway
The obvious drink-word names are taken by apps that do the same thing: Tally, Sipwise, Pourly, Permille, Promille variants, Sip Count and Måttfull. The searches found no same-name app for Klunk, Promilla, Pacely, Sipline, Mille, Glasskoll, or a Lagom app for drinking (other Lagom apps do exist).

### Cited Findings
**Rejected because there is a direct conflict:**
- Tally: "Tally - drinks counter" (Matthias Aigner) is on the App Store, released Jul 2022, rated 18+, and works as a beer and drinks tally. It is also on Android as at.MA.bierliste — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1621162112); [apkcombo](https://apkcombo.com/tally-drinks-counter/at.MA.bierliste/)
- Sipwise: "Sipwise - Drink Less Alcohol" (Andrew Okamoto) is a mindful-drinking app released Jan 2026, so it is the same niche. Sipwise GmbH is also a Vienna VoIP vendor with a "sip:phone" app — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6755543941); [App Store sip:phone](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1098176019); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/57347-56)
- Pourly: "Pourly - Bar Cost Calculator" is on iOS and was updated Jul 2026 — [AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/pourly-bar-cost-calculator/id6778648057)
- Permille: an iPhone app called "Permille" uses Widmark BAC, a traffic light, time-to-zero and local storage. That is nearly the same feature set as Alcometer — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/th/app/permille/id6787531362)

### Inferences
- If Promilla or Mille is chosen, the App Store title needs a distinguishing subtitle, because both share the "promille" root with at least three existing BAC apps.
- Lagom has no drink-app conflict, but searching for it in the store will surface unrelated Lagom apps, so it would be hard to find.

### Gaps
- The App Store and Google Play search pages are JS-rendered and were not queried directly. Coverage comes from web search indexing of store listings (apps.apple.com, mwm.ai, appgoblin, apkcombo), so new or low-traffic apps, especially on Google Play, may be missed.
- No Google Play check was done for Klunk, Pacely, Sipline, Mille, Promilla, Glasskoll or Lagom.

## Is there an obvious trademark conflict (EUIPO/TMview, USPTO, PRV; classes 9, 42, 44)?

### Takeaway
Not verified for any candidate. TMview, EUIPO eSearch, the USPTO tmsearch tool and PRV's search are interactive JS apps that were not queried. One web search for a LAGOM EU class 9 mark found nothing. The risks below come from general knowledge and still need a manual register search.

### Cited Findings
- A web search for "LAGOM trademark EUIPO class 9 software" returned only unrelated UK Trade Mark Journal entries and no LAGOM record. That is not evidence that no mark exists — [UK IPO TMJ example](https://www.ipo.gov.uk/t-tmj/tm-journals/2026-030/WO0000001927267.html)
- "Sipwise" is the trade name of Sipwise GmbH (VoIP software), so a class 9 or 38 conflict is likely. It is already rejected — [advantageaustria](https://www.advantageaustria.org/sn/company/fr/sipwise-gmbh)

### Inferences
- Lagom, Promilla, Mille and Pacely are descriptive or near-descriptive. "Lagom" is a common Swedish adjective, "promille" is the Swedish and German word for ‰, and "mille" means thousand in Italian and French. They would be hard to register as word marks, especially in Sweden and the EU, and easy for others to use. A stylised or figurative mark would be needed.
- Klunk (Swedish onomatopoeia for "gulp") and Sipline are the most distinctive of the survivors, so they are the most protectable for these classes.
- Skål: AB InBev's SKOL beer brand is a likely class 32 senior mark, and an alcohol-adjacent app could draw an objection. This is unsourced inference, which is one more reason Skål was dropped.

### Gaps
- No register results for any candidate in TMview, USPTO or PRV. Next step: run exact and fuzzy word searches for KLUNK, PROMILLA, PACELY, SIPLINE, MILLE and LAGOM in classes 9, 42 and 44 on tmview.org, tmsearch.uspto.gov and the PRV search (search.prv.se).

## Meaning and fit (pronunciation, EN/SV connotations, App Store risk, genericness)

### Takeaway
Klunk and Promilla fit best on both languages and the brand. Pacely and Sipline are clean in English but bland. Mille is elegant but weak. Lagom is the best fit in meaning (moderation), but it is generic and crowded. Glasskoll has a Swedish pitfall.

### Cited Findings
- Lagom is widely understood abroad as the Swedish idea of "not too little, not too much", from books by Brantmark and Dunne. Writing on the concept explicitly ties it to avoiding excess alcohol — [library catalog](https://catalog.northloganlibrary.org/OverDrive/6dce9cc7-3d4f-4c3d-9678-7db43f7cdd71/Home); [sleepopolis](https://sleepopolis.com/?p=146174)
- The comparable apps (Permille, Mypromille, Måttfull) all carry disclaimers that they are estimates and not for driving decisions, which is the framing expected in this category — [Permille](https://apps.apple.com/th/app/permille/id6787531362); [Mypromille](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6476889220); [Måttfull](https://apps.apple.com/se/app/m%C3%A5ttfull/id1532876104)

### Per-candidate assessment (inference)
1. **Klunk**: Swedish for "gulp/swig" ("en klunk vin"). Easy to say in English. In English it can read as "clunk", playful and a bit clumsy, which fits a neon, tongue-in-cheek tone. It has no drive or BAC implication. It does suggest drinking, which carries mild App Store 17+/18+ "references to alcohol" rating risk, but no more than any drink tracker. Distinctive, so the most protectable. Domain: klunkapp.com.
2. **Promilla**: a blend of promille + -a. Swedish and German users read ‰ at once, while English users may not know "promille". The root is shared with Mypromille, Meine Promille and Permille, and it is weak as a trademark. It does not imply driving, but avoid "under limit" copy. Domains: promilla.app and promilla.se look unregistered.
3. **Pacely**: from "pace". Clear in English and readable in Swedish. It frames pacing positively and has no slang problems. Moderately generic in a crowded pacing niche (Pound, Drink Pacing Journal). Domain: pacely.app.
4. **Sipline**: sip + timeline/line. It reads naturally in English and is easy in Swedish. Fairly distinctive. Faint echoes of "Skiline", "Spine" and "pipeline". sipline.com is likely for sale (Afternic), and .app is taken.
5. **Mille**: short and stylish (‰ = per mille). It is also a common first and surname (French "mille" = thousand). Very weak trademark and hard to search for. The domains are premium or parked; millebac.app is an alternative.
6. **Lagom**: the best match for meaning (moderation, pacing, Swedish origin) and internationally known. But it is generic, used by many brands and five-plus App Store apps, and hard to register. Use it only as part of a compound, e.g. "Lagom Tonight" or "LagomDrinks".
7. (Conditional) **Glasskoll**: intended as "glass check", but in Swedish "glass" means *ice cream*, so it reads as "ice-cream check". "Glaskoll" (one s) fixes that but was not checked in the stores or DNS. Keep only if it is re-vetted.
- Dropped on fit: Skål (clashes with SKOL beer and an old IKEA app; å is awkward internationally), Måtta (å; Systembolaget's Måttfull), Tonight and Nightcap (generic, and the names are taken).

### Gaps
- No native-speaker or slang-dictionary check was done for English slang meanings of "klunk" or "sipline". Urban Dictionary was not consulted.
- No check of App Store review guidelines text was done for this note. The rating-risk comments are general knowledge, not a cited guideline.
