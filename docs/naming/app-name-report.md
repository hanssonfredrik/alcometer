# Rename Alcometer to Ebbglow, Klunk or Pacely

> **Decision (2026-10-20):** the app was renamed to **Promilla** (candidate 9
> below), chosen by the owner over the top-3 recommendation. Its risks as
> recorded here still apply: it is descriptive (weak to trademark), it sits
> next to Mypromille / Meine Promille / Permille, and it names the promille
> estimate App Review has rejected in other apps. Still to do by hand: the
> TMview / EUIPO / PRV / USPTO checks in the table at the end, and securing
> promilla.app / promilla.se (promilla.com is taken).

**The best replacement for "Alcometer" is Ebbglow, with Klunk and Pacely as strong alternatives.** All three passed the store checks we ran with no same-name app found, none implies measuring anything or fitness to drive, and each has at least one plausible unregistered domain (ebbglow.com and ebbglow.app, klunkapp.com/.app, pacely.app). The rename matters for more than looks. "Alcometer" is close to the Norwegian and Danish word for a police breathalyser ("alkometer"). It also sits next to Lion Laboratories' ALCOLMETER breathalyser trademark, and it advertises the one feature Apple keeps rejecting: **software-only BAC calculators are being turned down under App Store Guideline 1.4 (Physical Harm)**, even when they carry disclaimers. A name about the evening, the log or the fading glow lets the App Store listing present the app as a drink journal and sober-streak tracker, with the estimate as a secondary, educational feature. That framing is what seems to survive review. Three caveats apply to everything below. **No trademark register was searched** (TMview, EUIPO, USPTO and PRV could not be queried). Store coverage comes from web-indexed listings, not searches inside the stores. Domain status comes from DNS and RDAP lookups, not WHOIS. The bundle id `se.venueve.neontracker` and the storage key `neontrk_v1` stay as they are. A rename changes only the display name and marketing.

## Apple rejects software-only BAC estimators, so the name must not sell one

The biggest naming finding is a regulatory one. Developers report repeated rejections under Guideline 1.4 with the wording that the app is **"marketed as a blood alcohol content calculator but does not have associated hardware"**. One German developer was told the app "can not be marked as a blood alcohol content calculator without the appropriate hardware", and other developers said renaming the value to "Geschätzter Promillewert" (estimated promille) would not help ([Apple Dev Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/775816)). The 2026 drink-logging app Abvy was **rejected six times** even after passing TestFlight beta review. An App Review appointment did not help. The two suggested ways out were to add approved hardware or to remove every BAC, time-to-sober and impairment output ([Apple Dev Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/848256)). Enforcement is inconsistent: Stay in the Blue and Virtual Bar are reportedly still listed, and at least one appeal was upheld ([Apple Dev Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/114265); [prolost](https://prolost.com/blog/drinkingbuddy)).

The listings that survive put the reflective framing first. Mypromille leads with "awareness and self-reflection" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6476889220)). Systembolaget's Måttfull says outright that it cannot measure promille ([App Store SE](https://apps.apple.com/se/app/m%C3%A5ttfull/id1532876104)). In practice, avoid any name built on Alco-/Alko-, -meter/-mätare, BAC, Promille, "breathalyser"/"blås", alkolås (ignition interlock) or "sober check". A name will not get the app approved by itself, because App Review judges what the app does. It can avoid inviting the objection, though, and it fits the project rule that no BAC band is ever phrased as fitness to drive. No Google Play rejections or policy text on BAC estimators were found, so the Android risk looks lower but is unconfirmed.

"Alcometer" also fails on its own terms. A Windows Phone BAC app already used the exact name, and a Russian "Алкометр" BAC project exists ([Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/alcometer-windows-phone-keeping-eye-adult-beverage-intake); [WebTranslateIt](https://webtranslateit.com/projects/9162-alcometer/locales/ru-RU..en/strings/5695489)). **Lion Laboratories has held ALCOLMETER since 1980** for apparatus that measures alcohol in breath, which is the same goods area ([Justia](https://trademark.justia.com/owners/lion-laboratories-limited-228197)). In Norwegian, "alkometer" means the hand-held device police use to check drivers ([SNL](https://snl.no/alkometer)).

## Most drink words are already taken by apps in this category

## Ebbglow leads because it is ownable, open and review-safe

**Ebbglow is the first choice.** It is the only candidate with both the .com and the .app apparently unregistered. It is coined, so it should be the easiest to clear and defend, and it captures both the neon look and the idea of a level fading without saying "measure". It works in Swedish and English with no bad meaning. A store title like "Ebbglow – Drink Journal & Streaks" puts the logging and sober-day framing first, which fits Guideline 1.4. **Klunk is second.** It is the most distinctive real word on the list, warmly Swedish, and has a light humour that suits the neon tone, though the domain would be klunkapp.com rather than the bare word. **Pacely is third.** It is the safest positioning for App Review, because the name is about pacing rather than levels, and pacely.app is open. It is also the most generic and sits in a busy pacing niche. Sipline is the runner-up for a pure "journal" positioning if buying the Afternic .com is acceptable. Ebbly is attractive but carries the Headspace Ebb risk.

None of these is cleared until the manual checks below are done. Domain availability can also change on any day, so register the chosen domain as soon as the trademark checks pass.

## Conclusion

The rename is less about finding a nice word than about taking the product's identity off the one feature Apple has repeatedly refused to approve. "Alcometer" tells App Review, Nordic users and Lion Laboratories' lawyers that this is a measuring device. Ebbglow, Klunk and Pacely tell them it is a journal for the evening. The decision that follows is about positioning rather than code: lead the iOS listing with logging, sober-day streaks and on-device privacy. Privacy is an unused angle in this category, and none of the names exploits it yet, so it can carry the subtitle. Present the ‰ estimate as educational. Be ready to ship an iOS build with the BAC readout reduced or hidden if Guideline 1.4 still bites. Android and the PWA can keep the full estimate, since no comparable Google enforcement was found.
