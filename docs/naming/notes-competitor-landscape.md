# Competitor naming landscape: drink trackers, BAC estimators, sobriety/streak apps

(These notes were meant for `.../scratchpad/research_notes/Alcometer app name candidates/competitor_landscape.md`. Plan mode was active, so they were written here instead.)

## Which apps track drinks, estimate BAC/promille, or track sobriety, and what are they called?

### Takeaway
The category is crowded and the names fall into a few reused buckets: "Sober ___" / "___ Sober", "Dry ___", "Drink ___", "Alco-/Alc-", "BAC ___", "Promille ___", and soft single words for "less drinking" ("Less", "Sipless", "Sipfulness", "Reframe", "Sunnyside", "Måttfull"). I could not get reliable install counts. The popularity notes below are rough, based on ratings and how often an app appears in roundups.

### Cited Findings
**Moderation / mindful-drinking trackers (US/UK)**
- Reframe: CBT-style quit/cut-back programme with paid coaching; about $99.99–$119.99/yr; one of the most-recommended (high profile) — [Oar Health](https://www.oarhealth.com/alcohol-use-disorder/treatment/reframe-vs-sunnyside-which-mindful-drinking-app); [ChoosingTherapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/best-mindful-drinking-apps/)
- Sunnyside: moderation app with logging by text message, dry days, money saved (high profile) — [Oar Health](https://www.oarhealth.com/alcohol-use-disorder/treatment/reframe-vs-sunnyside-which-mindful-drinking-app)
- Try Dry: free app from Alcohol Change UK, the charity behind Dry January; tracks units, money and calories, with a "drank as planned" category (high profile in the UK) — [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2024/01/05/mindful-drinking-apps-dry-january/); [ChoosingTherapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/best-mindful-drinking-apps/)
- MyDrinkaware – Alcohol Tracker (Drinkaware, UK): free unit tracker with goals, drink-free days and sleep — [App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/mydrinkaware-alcohol-tracker/id901389586)

### Inferences
- "Promille" is the default store keyword in the Nordic and German markets and is heavily used in app names (Promillekoll, Mypromille, Promilly, Promille Rechner, Promillerechner). Swedish public-health brands (Systembolaget) have moved away from promille estimates towards reflection ("Måttfull").
- "Sober" and "Dry" names cluster around streak and quit apps. "Sip" (Sipless, Sipfulness) and "Less" are a newer cluster of soft moderation names.
- The privacy and local-only angle is rare in names. Only "Sober Up" mentions on-device data, and only in its description.

### Gaps
- No reliable install or download counts (Play Store pages and Sensor Tower were not accessible). Popularity is inferred from roundups and rating counts.
- Finnish market: no Finnish-language promille app was found ("promillelaskuri" search returned none).
- Current availability of Promillekoll, ForFuld and Drikkevett in 2026 is unconfirmed.
- Did not verify the existence of other plausible names (e.g. "Drinkly", "Untoxicated"); the search returned nothing.

## Is "Alcometer" already used by other apps or devices?

### Takeaway
Yes. In several languages "Alcometer" or a close spelling is a common noun for a breathalyser. There was an "Alcometer" BAC app on Windows Phone, a Russian "Алкометр" app project, and Lion Laboratories holds the near-identical breathalyser trademark ALCOLMETER. The name is weak to defend and suggests a measuring device, which is exactly what App Review objects to.

### Cited Findings
- Windows Phone app "Alcometer" (v4.0.1.6): Widmark BAC estimate with a not-for-driving disclaimer — [Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/alcometer-windows-phone-keeping-eye-adult-beverage-intake); [Softpedia](https://mobile.softpedia.com/windows-phone/Alcometer-Windows-Phone-Review-3220.html); [AAWP](https://allaboutwindowsphone.com/flow/item/18868_Calculate_blood_alcohol_level_.php)
- A Russian app project "Alcometer" (Алкометр, Ruslan Valiahmetov) tracks BAC in promille with a "back to zero" notification — [WebTranslateIt](https://webtranslateit.com/projects/9162-alcometer/locales/ru-RU..en/strings/5695489)
- ALCOLMETER is a US trademark filed by Lion Laboratories in 1980 (serial 73256348) for apparatus that detects or measures alcohol in breath; it is still used on the Lion Alcolmeter® 700. Lion also holds ALCOSENTRY, ALCOPROBE and ALCOCAL — [Justia](https://trademark.justia.com/owners/lion-laboratories-limited-228197); [CMM](https://cmm.com.au/product/lion-700-breathalyser/)
- Norwegian/Danish "alkometer" means a hand-held device for promille checks on drivers (SNL); Danish police devices are also called "alkometer" — [SNL](https://snl.no/alkometer); [skagenonline](https://www.skagenonline.dk/faq/hvor-praecise-er-politiets-alkometer)
- Searches found no current iOS or Google Play listing named exactly "Alcometer" — [search result summary, Windows Central et al. above]

### Inferences
- "Alcometer" ≈ "alkometer" (NO/DK breathalyser), "alkometri" (FI) and "алкометр" (RU). For Nordic users it reads as "breathalyser", which signals measurement and roadside driving checks.
- The closeness to the ALCOLMETER mark in the same goods area (alcohol measurement) is a trademark risk, especially if the app adds any hardware or measurement features.

### Gaps
- Current live status of the ALCOLMETER registration and any EUIPO/PRV (Swedish) marks for "Alcometer"/"Alkometer" were not checked.
- Could not search the Play Store directly for exact-match listings.

## Which name roots are saturated, and which are underused?

### Takeaway
Saturated: Sober-, Dry-, Drink-, Alco-/Alc-, BAC-, Promille-, "-Tracker", "-Counter", "Sip-", "Less". Underused: the night or evening (as opposed to the drink), light and glow imagery tied to the neon look, the morning or "sober by" moment, the per-mille sign (‰), and privacy or local-only words.

### Cited Findings
- Sober-: I Am Sober, Sober Time, Sobriety Counter, Sober Tracker, Sober Up, Drink & Sober Tracker, Sipless: Sobriety Tracker — [sources above]
- Dry-: Try Dry, DryCount, The Dry — [sources above]
- Drink-: DrinkControl, MyDrinkaware, DrinkWise, Drikkevett, Drink & Sober Tracker — [sources above]
- Alco-/Alc-: AlcoDroid, AlcDroid, AlcoTrack, Alcolator, Alcometer, plus Lion's ALCOLMETER/ALCOSENTRY/ALCOPROBE/ALCOCAL marks — [sources above]
- BAC-: BAC Tracker, BACtrack, iBAC — [sources above]
- Promille-: Promillekoll, Mypromille, Promilly, Promille Rechner, AlcoTrack: Promillerechner — [sources above]
- Soft moderation words: Less, Sipless, Sipfulness, Reframe, Sunnyside, Måttfull, Abvy — [sources above]

### Inferences
- Nearly every store title adds a generic suffix ("– Alcohol Tracker", "– Sobriety Counter", "– Drink tracker"). A short, distinctive brand word plus a descriptive subtitle is the norm, and the brand word is where differentiation happens.
- Possible open space (my inference; collisions not verified): night or evening words, glow/neon/light imagery, the "until sober" or morning moment, the ‰ glyph, privacy or "local" words, and Swedish words other than "promille" or "alko".

### Gaps
- Each candidate still needs its own exact-match store and trademark search. This survey cannot rule out collisions for names that weren't searched.

## Regulatory and naming sensitivities

### Takeaway
This is the biggest risk. Apple App Review has repeatedly rejected software-only BAC/promille estimators under Guideline 1.4 (Physical Harm), with the reason "marketed as a blood alcohol content calculator but does not have associated hardware". Disclaimers and changing the wording to "estimate" have not been enough. A name built on Alco-/-meter/BAC/Promille draws attention to exactly that function.

### Cited Findings
- German developer: rejected under 1.4 for showing "Promillewert"; App Review said the app "can not be marked as a blood alcohol content calculator without the appropriate hardware"; community view is that changing to "Geschätzter Promillewert" will not help — [Apple Dev Forums 775816](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/775816)
- Abvy (2026): rejected six times under 1.4 even though TestFlight/Beta Review approved; wording: "marketed as a blood alcohol content calculator but does not have associated hardware... apps that rely solely on software... are not appropriate"; App Review appointment didn't help; appeal pending. Suggested ways out: use approved hardware, or remove all BAC, sobriety-time and impairment outputs — [Apple Dev Forums 848256](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/848256)
- Other 1.4 BAC rejections; enforcement is inconsistent and some similar apps remain listed; one developer's appeal was upheld — [Apple Dev Forums 114265](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/114265); [658884](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/658884); [prolost DrinkingBuddy](https://prolost.com/blog/drinkingbuddy)
- Listings that survive put "awareness/self-reflection", "educational only" and "not for driving decisions" up front (Mypromille); Systembolaget's Måttfull explicitly says it cannot measure promille — [Mypromille](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6476889220); [Måttfull](https://apps.apple.com/se/app/m%C3%A5ttfull/id1532876104)
- "Breathalyzer" is a genericized trademark (Borkenstein's Breathalyzer brand) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathalyzer)

### Inferences
- Avoid names that imply measurement or driving fitness: -meter/-mätare, Alko-/Alco-, BAC, Promille, breathalyser/"blås", alkolås (ignition interlock), "safe to drive", "sober check". This fits the project rule never to phrase a band as fitness to drive.
- A name about logging, the evening or streaks lets the store listing present the app as a drink journal and streak tracker, with the estimate as a secondary educational feature. That is the presentation that seems to pass review. A rename alone won't guarantee approval, because App Review looks at the function too.

### Gaps
- No official Apple text names BAC calculators. The evidence is developer reports only.
- No Google Play policy text on BAC estimators was found, and no Google rejections were found. Google risk is unknown, apparently lower.
- EU/Swedish legal constraints on the marketing of alcohol-related apps (e.g. the Swedish Alcohol Act's marketing rules) were not researched.
